### [SIGNIFICANT] eMudhra exits the TLS certificate business; Chrome's root removal lands today
- **Date:** eMudhra's exit announcement Oct 22, 2026; Chrome Root Store removal of emSign ECC Root CA - G3 rolling out today, Oct 27, 2026
- **Source:** CSOonline, https://www.csoonline.com/article/4238338/google-mozilla-revoke-emudhra-intermediates-after-private-key-compromise.html ; The Hacker News, https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/eight-emudhra-ca-keys-compromised.html ; YourStory, https://yourstory.com/2026/10/emudhra-faces-scrutiny-after-weak-certificate-keys-raise-web-security-concerns
- **Summary:** Following the Oct 17-20 disclosure that eight of eMudhra's intermediate CA private keys were trivially recoverable (logged here 2026-10-26), the fallout has escalated: on Oct 22 eMudhra said it is exiting the TLS certificate business entirely, and today Chrome's removal of eMudhra's emSign ECC Root CA - G3 from its root store is rolling out, which will break any site whose certificate still chains to that root unless it has already switched to another trusted chain. eMudhra estimated roughly 30,000 certificates under the G3 root needed to move (97% issued via ACME, so largely automatable), and said Google is separately investigating whether its remaining roots should stay trusted. A publicly trusted CA voluntarily exiting the TLS market following a key-compromise incident is a rare, market-moving event for the CA ecosystem — a strong real-world data point for the watchlist's crypto-agility and automated-CLM pitch (the affected certs, mostly ACME-issued, are exactly the kind of inventory that automated tools from Keyfactor, AppViewX, and CyberArk Certificate Manager are built to find and rotate quickly when a CA goes bad).
