### [SIGNIFICANT] eMudhra fiasco widens: eight more intermediate CA certificates revoked (16 total), Microsoft pulls trust
- **Date:** Second batch (RSA CAs) revoked Nov 7, 2026 with reason code cessationOfOperation; widely reported Nov 11, 2026
- **Source:** BleepingComputer, https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/emudhra-revokes-8-more-ca-certificates-after-private-key-mishap ; The Hacker News, https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/emudhra-revokes-8-more-cas-it.html ; itnews.com.au, https://www.itnews.com.au/news/emudhra-fiasco-widens-eight-more-cas-revoked-microsoft-pulls-trust-630303
- **Summary:** Following the October disclosure that eight eMudhra intermediate CA keys were trivially recoverable (logged 2026-10-26/27), eMudhra's own investigation found improper private-key handling in a separate batch of eight RSA intermediate CAs, which it revoked Nov 7 — bringing the total affected intermediates to 16. Unlike the first batch (revoked with caCompromise, keys recoverable in under a second), this second batch was revoked with cessationOfOperation, and reporting differs on the exact cause: some sources say the keys themselves weren't weakened but eMudhra couldn't prove a key transfer followed policy; eMudhra says it found no evidence of malicious exploitation and that affected customer certificates have been replaced or transferred. Microsoft has now pulled trust as well, following Chrome, Firefox, and Apple's earlier actions on the first batch. **Why this matters:** this confirms the eMudhra incident is a broader organizational/process failure at the CA rather than a one-off key-generation bug, reinforcing the case for automated certificate/key lifecycle management (the watchlist's core pitch) and underscoring why eMudhra's exit from the public TLS CA business (announced Oct 22) was the right call. No new developments on the DigiCert archived-data breach this week.
