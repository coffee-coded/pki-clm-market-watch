### [SIGNIFICANT] eMudhra CA private-key compromise: eight intermediate CA keys were trivially recoverable, browsers block certs, Chrome removing eMudhra root
- **Date:** Discovered by researcher "Wayne" Oct 17, 2026; made public by Mozilla's Dennis Jackson on dev-security-policy Oct 20, 2026; Chrome Root Store removal of eMudhra's emSign ECC Root CA - G3 rolling out Oct 27, 2026
- **Source:** The Hacker News, https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/eight-emudhra-ca-keys-compromised.html ; CSOonline, https://www.csoonline.com/article/4238338/google-mozilla-revoke-emudhra-intermediates-after-private-key-compromise.html ; BleepingComputer, https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/mozilla-google-apple-block-compromised-emudhra-ca-certificates ; Mozilla dev-security-policy / Bugzilla 2083449
- **Summary:** Eight intermediate CA certificates issued by India's eMudhra (a publicly trusted CA, cross-signed by Sectigo) turned out to have private keys that were trivially recoverable — not stolen, but generated with a script error that produced P-384 keys with all-zero bytes except the last (one recovered key's value was literally 4; others were 56 and 115). Six of the eight intermediates issue TLS certificates, two are for code signing. eMudhra says most were never used in production and it found no evidence of fraudulently issued end-entity certificates in public CT logs, but Chrome, Firefox, Apple, and Microsoft all blocked the eight certificates within hours of disclosure (via CRLSet/OneCRL/CRLite — no software update required), and Google is removing eMudhra's emSign ECC Root CA - G3 entirely from the Chrome Root Store on Oct 27. The affected intermediates trace back to a batch of 24 generated April 30, 2025. **Why this matters for the watchlist:** this is a second major CA-trust-chain integrity incident in under a month (following the Sept ccTLD DNS-hijack/fraudulent-Google-cert story logged 2026-10-15) — this time the failure is in the CA's own key-generation process rather than DNS validation, but it's the same underlying theme: the public-CA trust model is only as strong as its weakest operational link. Residual risk sits in non-browser software (Go binaries, anything using Python's certifi bundle, embedded devices) that doesn't check revocation data and may keep trusting the affected chain until updated — directly relevant to the watchlist's broader CLM/PKI-infrastructure-integrity and crypto-agility themes, and a concrete real-world argument for the kind of automated certificate/key lifecycle management Keyfactor, AppViewX, and CyberArk Certificate Manager all sell.
