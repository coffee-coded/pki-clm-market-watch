### [SIGNIFICANT] DigiCert discloses breach of archived customer data, possibly including identity documents
- **Date:** Unauthorized access window June 27–July 2, 2026; DigiCert confirmed scope internally Oct 19, 2026; publicly disclosed early November 2026
- **Source:** BleepingComputer, https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/digicert-data-breach-exposes-customer-information-identity-documents ; SecurityWeek, https://securityweek.com/digicert-data-breach-impacts-business-customer-information ; The Register, https://www.theregister.com/a/5308097 ; eSecurity Planet, https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/news-digicert-breach-customer-data-id-documents/
- **Summary:** DigiCert disclosed that an unauthorized account read a subset of its archived customer records, tied to a February 2025 server migration that left an internal business system reachable from the internet with an authorization flaw. The access window was June 27–July 2, 2026 (account blocked July 2); DigiCert needed until October to determine the account had actually touched archived customer contact data (names, emails, phone numbers, business addresses), confirming this Oct 19. The Register reports exposed data may also include identity documents (passports, driver's licenses) for a "small minority" of accounts — a more severe characterization than DigiCert's own framing. DigiCert says its CA issuance/validation systems were not affected and no certificate reissuance is required; this is a business-systems breach, not a PKI-trust-chain incident. A disclosed breach involving a watchlist company (DigiCert, secondary vendor) meets this tracker's SIGNIFICANT bar, though the practical CLM/PKI impact appears limited — worth distinguishing from the September DigiCert breach (fraudulent EV code-signing certs) as a separate incident.
