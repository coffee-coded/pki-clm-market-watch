### [SIGNIFICANT] Attackers hijacked three ccTLD registries (.gh, .sl, .as) to fraudulently obtain real TLS certificates for Google/YouTube domains
- **Date:** Certificates issued Sept 22–27, 2026; Google disclosed Oct 6, 2026; revocations completed by Oct 1–7, 2026; surfaced in this tracker today, 2026-10-15
- **Source:** The Hacker News, https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/attackers-hijack-gh-sl-and-as.html ; Security Boulevard, https://securityboulevard.com/2026/10/attackers-hijack-3-country-code-registries-obtain-google-certificates/ ; Google Trust Services status page, https://status.pki.goog/
- **Summary:** Attackers compromised the operators of three country-code domain registries — .gh (Ghana), .sl (Sierra Leone), and .as (American Samoa) — and altered authoritative DNS records to pass domain-control validation checks. This let them fraudulently obtain 12 legitimate, trusted TLS certificates covering 7 Google/YouTube domain names: Let's Encrypt issued 11, ZeroSSL issued 1. Google was not itself breached — the CAs were fooled by legitimate-looking DNS records at the hijacked registries. Chrome auto-blocked the identified certificates via CRLSets, and all certificates were revoked (the .gh batch and the ZeroSSL cert by Sept 26, the remaining nine by Oct 1). Google's disclosure doesn't name the attackers, explain the registry compromise method, give a complete list of affected domains, or confirm whether the certs were used for active interception. **Why this matters for the watchlist:** this is a textbook case of the domain-validation trust-chain failure mode that underpins the entire public-CA ecosystem (Let's Encrypt, DigiCert, Sectigo, GlobalSign, etc. all rely on the same DNS/HTTP validation model) — a hijacked registry can fool any CA's automated DV checks regardless of vendor. Directly relevant to this watchlist's "Let's Encrypt / ISRG (market/standards signal)" line and the broader CLM/PKI-infrastructure-integrity topic. Missed for over a week — another reminder that standalone CA security incidents need deliberate sweeps, since they don't surface through vendor-PR-style searches on the primary three companies.
