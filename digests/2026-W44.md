## Significant

### eMudhra CA private-key compromise: eight intermediate CA keys trivially recoverable, Chrome removing eMudhra root Oct 27
Eight intermediate CA certificates from India's eMudhra (cross-signed by
Sectigo) turned out to have private keys that were trivially recoverable —
a 2025 key-generation script error produced P-384 keys with almost all
zero bytes (one recovered key's value was literally 4). Discovered Oct 17,
made public by Mozilla Oct 20; Chrome, Firefox, Apple, and Microsoft all
blocked the eight certificates within hours via CRLSet/OneCRL/CRLite, and
Google is removing eMudhra's emSign ECC Root CA - G3 entirely from the
Chrome Root Store on Oct 27. No fraudulently issued end-entity certificates
have been found in public CT logs. This is the second major CA-trust-chain
integrity incident in under a month, after the September ccTLD DNS-hijack
story (logged 2026-10-15) — different failure mode (key generation vs. DNS
validation), same underlying lesson about the fragility of the public-CA
trust model and the case for automated certificate/key lifecycle tooling.

## Process note

The eMudhra incident is the first major CA-security story this tracker has
caught within days of disclosure rather than weeks later — a good sign for
the dedicated "CA incident" sweep query, though it may just reflect how
widely covered this particular story was (Hacker News, CSOonline,
BleepingComputer, Mozilla's own mailing list all carried it same-week).

pattern of primary- and secondary-vendor leadership/personnel news lagging
weeks to months behind its actual announcement date.
