## Significant

### Attackers hijacked three ccTLD registries (.gh, .sl, .as) to fraudulently obtain real TLS certificates for Google/YouTube domains
Attackers compromised the operators of three country-code domain registries
and altered authoritative DNS records to pass domain-control validation,
fraudulently obtaining 12 trusted TLS certificates for 7 Google/YouTube
domains (11 from Let's Encrypt, 1 from ZeroSSL). Google itself was not
breached — the CAs were fooled by legitimate-looking DNS records at the
hijacked registries. All 12 certificates were revoked by Oct 1–7; Google
disclosed Oct 6; this tracker caught it Oct 15. This is a textbook case of
the domain-validation trust-chain failure mode underlying the entire
public-CA ecosystem — any CA relying on DNS/HTTP validation (Let's Encrypt,
DigiCert, Sectigo, GlobalSign, etc.) is exposed to the same failure mode
regardless of vendor.
